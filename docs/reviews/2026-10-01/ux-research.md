# Independent UX and customer-research review

**Verdict.** Thesis has a coherent research-to-review concept, but its proposed first session asks beginners to practise a fairly sophisticated investment discipline before demonstrating enough immediate value. The next design iteration should prove that a newcomer can understand one investment question and its uncertainty, then decide whether structured monitoring warrants additional effort. This is an AI design review of planning documents, not observed usability or customer evidence. Application integration remains unimplemented ([README, lines 27–29](../../../README.md)). Recommendations below are proposals, not approvals to implement or recruit.

**Two strengths worth preserving.** First, the brief separates performance, expectations, support and contrary evidence, with source detail available on demand. This gives the interface a useful structure without requiring good prompting ([UX, Company brief, lines 50–61](../../archive/planning/ux-and-user-journey.md)). Second, the return journey preserves previous reasoning, evidence and uncertainty, permits an unresolved conclusion, and avoids turning a favourable condition into a buy instruction. That is a stronger foundation for trust than a single aggregate verdict ([UX, Returning user, lines 81–97](../../archive/planning/ux-and-user-journey.md)).

**Five issues to resolve.**

1. **The activation definition contradicts valid research outcomes.** The UX permits a draft or concluding that an idea is unconvincing, but the product plan requires an approved thesis with a condition for first-session success and activation ([UX, line 40](../../archive/planning/ux-and-user-journey.md); [Product, lines 19–21 and 77](../../archive/planning/product-and-launch-plan.md)). This creates pressure to manufacture a monitorable belief and excludes useful rejection from success. Separate research activation—understanding a claim, uncertainty and next step—from monitoring activation. Let “I need more information” and “I am not pursuing this” be complete outcomes. Keep monitored ideas subject to the existing minimum-condition contract; draft persistence needs an explicit design because the proposed save service validates at least one condition ([Database, line 85](../../archive/planning/database-design.md)).

2. **Plain language does not remove the intellectual burden of condition authoring.** Asking beginners to specify metric, threshold, period, scope and deadline assumes they know what evidence would meaningfully test their reasoning ([UX, Idea editor, lines 65–73](../../archive/planning/ux-and-user-journey.md)). Reviewing a generated threshold may become accepting an anchor rather than exercising judgment. Begin with one relevant proposed condition, showing its rationale, what it can and cannot establish, and what evidence would leave it unresolved. Offer “I cannot define this yet.” Reveal detailed editing only on request. Before approval, show a concrete matching and non-matching example. Test understanding independently of clicking Save; an explicit review screen alone does not demonstrate informed authorship.

3. **The state model is safer than average but still underspecified for comprehension.** Users must distinguish review status, favourable/adverse condition results, conflicting evidence and coverage ([UX, lines 85–95 and 101–110](../../archive/planning/ux-and-user-journey.md)). Without a fixed hierarchy, “Condition met” can dominate “Data unavailable.” Show the affected question, observation and limitation together; attach coverage to each result, not only the company header. Explain what “material” means operationally before using it in quiet-state copy. Replace the broad accessibility checklist with acceptance cases: keyboard access and focus return for source panels, announced loading/error changes, labelled status text, and readable before/after comparisons on narrow screens and enlarged text ([UX, line 131](../../archive/planning/ux-and-user-journey.md)). These are requirements to verify, not claims of current compliance.

4. **The return flow risks manufacturing work and pressuring users to reaffirm a belief.** Weekly reviews, first-week adjustment and day-30 revisits are scheduled around product engagement rather than established user needs ([Product, lines 23–33](../../archive/planning/product-and-launch-plan.md)). “Keep my idea” also conflates acknowledging an update with deciding the idea remains sound. Use “Mark reviewed,” optional revision and “Need more information.” A quiet visit should end successfully without a forced confirmation. Before adding delivery channels, define grouping of repeated stories, multiple affected conditions, corrections and persistent outages. Database event deduplication prevents duplicate records; it does not establish that separate events are meaningfully different to a user ([Database, lines 48 and 91](../../archive/planning/database-design.md)).

5. **The research design may reward following the script rather than solving the underlying problem.** The proposed task leads participants through every intended step, while the pilot combines heterogeneous beginners and measures retention among people who completed the demanding activation flow ([UX, lines 127–129](../../archive/planning/ux-and-user-journey.md); [Product, lines 65–84](../../archive/planning/product-and-launch-plan.md)). Keep the activated-user retention measure, but also report the full recruited cohort, non-completers and reasons for stopping. Segment by recent research behaviour and experience, not just student/job status. Interview early departures as well as end-of-pilot survivors. First investigate a recent real research episode and existing workaround; showing the concept first can shape the answer toward the proposed solution.

**A smaller proposed journey.** First visit: choose a company or labelled sample; optionally pick one question; read a compact answer with one supporting fact, one challenge and one explicit unknown; inspect a source. Then choose “Continue researching,” “Save this question,” or “Not pursuing.” Offer structured monitoring only when the user wants it, with one reviewed condition and visible coverage. Ask for sign-in at private saving and notification preferences after monitoring is established. Preserve the input across sign-in.

Return visit: show the saved question and either one grouped evidence change or an honest quiet/coverage state. Open the change beside the previous observation and the user's reasoning. Allow acknowledgement, revision or unresolved status; preserve version history. Do not require a freshly authored thesis or repeated approval of unchanged content.

**Three proposed experiments and criteria.**

- Compare the current thesis-first flow with the smaller journey using recorded fictional material, alternating presentation order. Ask participants to explain the reported fact, proposed assumption and unresolved question without coaching. Proposed criterion: no critical misunderstanding is concealed by a successful save. Record assistance and completion time; five minutes remains a hypothesis, not a pass mark.
- Test returns containing unchanged evidence, a genuine adverse change, duplicate reporting, a correction and an outage. Proposed criterion: participants distinguish quiet monitoring from failed coverage, retain the mixed picture, and avoid treating acknowledgement as resolving risk. Repeat essential tasks with keyboard, screen-reader and phone users; do not infer accessibility from desktop completion.
- In a later authorised longitudinal study, compare use against each person's existing research routine. Proposed criterion: document concrete episodes where Thesis reduced effort or clarified uncertainty, alongside unnecessary interruptions and non-use. Separate prompted from self-initiated visits and genuine opportunities to review from quiet periods. Logins, alert opens and stated subscription interest are insufficient evidence of recurring value.

**Unresolved assumptions.** Beginners may prefer an answer over maintaining a thesis; suggested conditions may increase confidence without comprehension; useful changes may occur too infrequently for weekly retention; sources may not support the questions users care about. The emotional descriptions, completion times, preferred cadence and willingness to pay all remain hypotheses. Resolve these before expanding the workflow or treating monitoring adoption as proof of trust.
