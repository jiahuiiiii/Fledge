# Product strategy review

Thesis has a credible product hypothesis: help an investor preserve their reasoning and recognise when new evidence challenges it. It is not yet a differentiated product or a validated business. I would pursue a smaller first product centred on reviewing an existing idea after an earnings release. Broad company research should support that task. The planning documents explicitly acknowledge their unimplemented status and lack of customer validation; this review supplies product judgments, not customer evidence.

Two strengths are worth preserving.

- **The research-to-review loop has a clear purpose.** “Why might this work?”, contrary evidence and changes since the last review connect acquisition with a plausible return occasion. The before/after screen could save the work of reconstructing an old argument. [Product promise, lines 5–7](../../archive/planning/product-and-launch-plan.md); [change review, lines 89–97](../../archive/planning/ux-and-user-journey.md).
- **The design treats uncertainty and user agency seriously.** Approved idea versions, separate coverage status, contradictory evidence and explicit approval of generated changes provide a sound foundation for trust. These are especially valuable when a company’s results are mixed. [Design boundaries, lines 25–30](../../../AGENTS.md); [returning-user states, lines 85–97](../../archive/planning/ux-and-user-journey.md).

My five priority issues follow.

1. **The initial audience describes demographics more clearly than a recurring job.** The plan includes students, interns and graduates who either have an idea or merely want to start researching. Those users have substantially different readiness and needs. Campus access identifies a recruitment channel; it does not establish which problem deserves a product. [Audience, lines 11–15](../../archive/planning/product-and-launch-plan.md).

   The consequence is ambiguous feedback: a curious beginner may appreciate explanations while never needing monitoring. My proposed initial user is a novice individual-stock investor already following two to five supported US operating companies and expecting to revisit an idea around results. Their job is: “Show me whether this release changes the reasons I was interested, with evidence I can inspect.” Include students and graduates who meet that behavioural definition, and compare them without presuming graduates will pay more.

2. **The current differentiation sits below a layer of commodity research features.** The architecture calls the structured brief distinctive, but fundamentals, summaries, source links and alerts already have substantial substitutes. [Methodology, lines 85–93](../../archive/planning/architecture.md).

   Koyfin advertises financials, estimates and watchlists in its free plan, and price, valuation and news alerts elsewhere in its product. Quartr advertises free mobile AI research across investor-relations material, traceable outputs and keyword alerts. These are observed vendor claims, not independently tested product quality. [Koyfin pricing](https://www.koyfin.com/pricing/), [Koyfin alerts](https://www.koyfin.com/features/alerts/), [Quartr mobile](https://quartr.com/products/mobile-app).

   The consequence is a weak reason to adopt another research interface. My inference is that Thesis’s strongest candidate advantage is the connection between **my approved reasoning, the exact new evidence and a recorded reassessment**. That is a testable workflow hypothesis; the reviewed pages do not establish competitors lack comparable functionality. Demonstrate this advantage against a source-linked generic summary plus the user’s existing notes before expanding research breadth.

3. **The product asks beginners to formalise an investment argument before proving they can do so meaningfully.** The first session should produce an approved thesis, while the editor asks users to review thresholds, periods, event scope, deadlines and evidence requirements. [First session, line 21](../../archive/planning/product-and-launch-plan.md); [idea editor, lines 67–73](../../archive/planning/ux-and-user-journey.md).

   Explicit approval alone does not demonstrate understanding. Users may accept plausible generated thresholds simply to finish, producing monitoring that precisely evaluates an argument they never held. Start with their existing sentence and one concrete question. Suggest at most a small number of supported conditions, require a brief explanation in their own words, and permit qualitative questions to remain unresolved. Measure comprehension separately from saving.

4. **Activation and retention partly contradict the intended value.** The UX correctly accepts rejecting an unconvincing idea, but activation requires saving an approved thesis. Week-four activity and weekly review are proposed despite an event-driven monitoring job. [Valid stopping points, line 40](../../archive/planning/ux-and-user-journey.md); [metrics, lines 77–84](../../archive/planning/product-and-launch-plan.md).

   This can classify a successful rejection as failure and reward unnecessary check-ins during quiet periods. Separate research activation—an evidence-supported pursued, rejected or unresolved conclusion—from monitoring activation. Add event-based return measurement: after a relevant covered development, did the user inspect it and record a reassessment? Keep time-based retention as supplementary evidence, and report users who received no relevant event separately.

5. **The first-product surface is larger than the unresolved value and cost assumptions justify.** Briefs, arbitrary conditions, chat, social narratives, estimates, valuation scenarios and multiple notification options create several products’ worth of expectations. Consensus may remain unavailable, while paid packaging and serving costs are unresolved. [Expectations and valuation, lines 89–93](../../archive/planning/architecture.md); [revenue, lines 47–54](../../archive/planning/product-and-launch-plan.md).

   The consequence is expensive integration before discovering what people repeatedly need. “Expectations” also risks implying insight into what a share price already reflects when the available evidence only describes management guidance or user beliefs. Initially label those categories exactly. Defer social ingestion, broad market explainers, arbitrary valuation scenarios, general chat and multiple delivery channels. Preserve sources, history and missing-data handling. Team ownership and reuse are already authorised; they are implementation advantages, not evidence that every inherited capability belongs in launch scope.

My minimum first-product recommendation is an **earnings review for a saved investment idea**: a bounded company set, one-page fundamentals brief, user reasoning, a few approved conditions, one relevant evidence update, and a before/after review with inspectable sources. Reuse the latest Kestrel screens and selected Deus acquisition capabilities. Begin with recorded fixtures, then permitted sources when authorised. Support in-app review and one optional digest. Make “what changed in my reasoning?” the demonstration’s centre.

Three proposed validation tests would guide expansion. These criteria are suggested decision rules, not established benchmarks or statistical proof; recruitment, collection and paid trials require their own authorised scope.

- **Existing problem test:** Interview 12 independent, behaviourally qualified users about their latest earnings review, using their actual workflow where authorised. Proposed pass criterion: at least eight demonstrate existing review behaviour and a specific difficulty recovering prior reasoning or connecting updates to it. Enthusiasm for an imagined app does not count.
- **Comparative usefulness test:** Counterbalance Thesis against a generic source-linked summary plus notes using identical recorded evidence. Proposed pass criterion: faster median completion, no increase in material interpretation errors, and at least eight of 12 users correctly explain the changed condition and remaining uncertainty without help. Failure should trigger simplification.
- **Return and payment test:** Observe users across two relevant event opportunities. Proposed pass criterion: at least six of ten review both without researcher prompting; subsequently, at least four accept a concrete paid continuation offer whose measured serving and support costs fit the price. Report independent users separately and avoid investment returns as the success measure.

These recommendations assume an individual-user product focused initially on supported US companies. Neither willingness to maintain structured ideas nor willingness to pay for ongoing review is established. Those are the main product risks to resolve before broader coverage.
